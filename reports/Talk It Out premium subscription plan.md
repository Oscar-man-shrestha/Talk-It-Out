# Sell teams first, keep safety free

Talk It Out should make its first money from a **Team plan that organisations buy on the web**: **₹149 per active member per month, or ₹99 a month on annual billing (excluding 18% GST)**, and $4 or $3 outside India. Anonymity, the AI tone rewrite, controller review, reporting, blocking and Catch up summaries stay free for everyone, permanently. What Team sells is what organisations already pay rivals for: room beyond the free limits, k-anonymous group insights, a weekly controller digest, templates, and later a web admin console. Nothing paid may change who can see an anonymous author. An individual **Plus** tier (₹99 a month, ₹499 a year; $2.99 or $14.99) should come third, not first. India and Southeast Asia convert only about **0.7% of installs to paid** and earn about **$0.11 per install by day 60**. At those rates, ₹50,000 a month from consumers needs roughly 100,000 installs, while the same income from teams is roughly 23–35 paying teams. Store rules point the same way. While the apps sell nothing, Android qualifies as a "consumption-only" app and iOS fits Apple's organisation-sales exception (guideline 3.1.3(c)). Once consumer in-app purchases exist, Team should also be offered as an in-app bundle, which keeps it within guideline 3.1.3(b). On the technical side, the Express/PostgreSQL backend has to become the one place that decides who has paid for what. It takes Razorpay events for web seats (Stripe is invite-only in India, and RevenueCat's own web billing does not serve India or B2B) and, later, RevenueCat events for store purchases. It also needs a "workspace" concept that the current schema lacks. Finally, the repo's existing pricing hypothesis needs three fixes. It puts already-free features (custom tone, summaries) behind the paywall. It sells a controller audit log that `AGENTS.md` rules out of scope for v1. And it starts the trial at a user's second group, although the free plan allows three groups.

## India-region installs earn about $0.11 each, while teams renew like business software

RevenueCat's 2026 benchmark covers more than 115,000 apps. Worldwide, freemium apps convert a median **2.1% of downloads to paid within 35 days**. Its India-plus-Southeast-Asia region (IN/SEA) converts **0.7%**, although another section of the same report says 1.4%. Trials there convert **15.2%, against 34.2% in North America**, and revenue per install reaches **$0.11 by day 60, against $0.55** ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). The store split matters for an audience that mostly uses Android: an IN/SEA iOS install earns $0.15 by day 60 and an Android install earns $0.04 (same source). Sensor Tower puts India's 2025 revenue per download at about **$0.03** ([TechCrunch, Apr 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/04/22/indias-app-market-is-booming-but-global-platforms-are-capturing-most-of-the-gains/)). The trend is real. Indian consumer app spending reached a record **$345M in Q2 2026, up 35% year on year**, and non-gaming apps made up 68% of revenue ([TechCrunch, Jul 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/31/india-is-starting-to-pay-for-apps-not-just-download-them/)). But it is growing from a very low base.

Retention makes the gap wider. For monthly plans, the first-renewal rate is lowest in Social & Lifestyle (**42%**) and highest in Business (**61%**). Business also leads on first yearly renewal at **40%**, against 23% for Productivity ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). In IN/SEA, only **46% of monthly and 22% of yearly subscribers renew even once**, and the region has the highest median refund rate, 7.7% (same source). An individual subscription for people who read a social Q&A feed lands in the weakest of these profiles. A seat plan bought by a startup or HR team lands in the strongest.

The revenue arithmetic follows. After 18% GST and Apple's 15% Small Business commission, a ₹99 monthly Plus sale nets roughly **₹71**. That is my estimate; Apple pays proceeds "minus applicable taxes" ([Apple: Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). The repo's go-to-market plan treats ₹50,000 in monthly recurring revenue (MRR) by month six as a strong signal ([GO_TO_MARKET.md](../docs/GO_TO_MARKET.md)). Reaching that with Plus takes about 700 paying subscribers. At a 0.7% install-to-paid rate, that is on the order of **100,000 installs before churn** (50,000 at the 1.4% figure). The same ₹50,000 from Team at ₹99–₹149 a seat is about **350–520 seats, or 23–35 teams of fifteen**, which one founder can close through direct outreach. The benchmarks also warn against relying on app-store discovery. Only **14.7% of new Business-category apps reach $1,000 MRR within two years**, and those that do take a median of 113 days ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026 Business](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026-business)). The top 10% of apps take 94.5% of subscription revenue ([Adapty 2026](https://adapty.io/blog/mobile-app-monetization-2026/)). Selling to teams through founder-led outreach and the web avoids that lottery.

There is a case for a consumer tier, but it comes later. Snap reported more than **30 million subscribers** across its paid products on 4 November 2026 ([Snap Newsroom](https://newsroom.snap.com/snapchat-subscription-offerings-surpass-30-million)). Telegram Premium passed 12 million in December 2024 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/23/pavel-durov-says-telegram-is-now-profitable)). LinkedIn Premium passed $2B a year, with nearly 40% of subscribers using its AI features ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/01/29/linkedin-passes-2b-in-premium-revenues-in-12-months-with-overall-revenues-up-9-on-the-year)). Each of them charges an audience that already exists at scale. Talk It Out's own plan calls the public feed the free top of the funnel that brings teams in ([STARTUP_PLAN.md](../docs/STARTUP_PLAN.md)). Charging feed users before the feed has an audience would buy a small consumer income at the cost of slower team acquisition.

## Rivals charge for anonymity and moderation, so Talk It Out must sell oversight instead

Talk It Out already gives away features that its rivals charge for. Polly reserves anonymity and privacy options for its Pro plan at $29 a month ([Polly pricing](https://www.polly.ai/pricing)). Mentimeter puts Q&A moderation (pre-approving questions, the closest thing to controller review) in Pro at $24.99 per presenter ([Mentimeter plans](https://www.mentimeter.com/plans)). Anonymous-reporting vendors charge organisations heavily: AllVoices' median buyer pays **$15,750 a year** ([Vendr](https://www.vendr.com/marketplace/allvoices)) and Spot lists $249–$999 a month ([Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/hr/spot-profile/)). Consumer workplace-anonymity apps go the other way. Blind keeps verified anonymous posting free and charges for an ad-free "Clean Feed" and for currency spent on extra DMs and premium chats ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id737534965); [TeamBlind forum](https://www.teamblind.com/post/does-blind-charge-qcdky3vk)). Fishbowl charges consumers nothing ([App Store IN](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id1005070636)). Keeping anonymity and review free is the right trust story, and the repo's plan already commits to it ([GO_TO_MARKET.md](../docs/GO_TO_MARKET.md)). The consequence is that the paid tier has to find its value in tools for the organisation.

The record of backlash shows where the line is. Twitter's 2023 move to restrict SMS two-factor login to paying subscribers was condemned as putting security behind a paywall ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2023/02/18/1158134620/twitter-blue-two-factor-authentication-2fa)). Meta Verified's paid impersonation protection drew lasting criticism from safety advocates ([Time](https://time.com/6257711/facebook-instagram-twitter-paid-verification)). NGL sold "hints" about who had sent an anonymous message for $9.99 a month. It ended in a **$4.5M redress plus $500K penalty** settlement with the FTC and the Los Angeles District Attorney, and a ban on serving users under 18 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/07/09/ftc-bans-ngl-from-offering-its-anonymous-social-app-to-minors)). Glassdoor added real names to anonymous profiles without consent, and users deleted their accounts ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/03/21/glassdoor-180-users-real-names-accounts-employers-trashed-them/)). Snapchat capped free Memories storage, got one-star reviews and users exporting their data, and had to offer a 12-month grace period ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/29/snapchat-caps-free-memory-storage-launches-paid-storage-plans)). The model that drew no backlash in these sources: the speaker's anonymity is free, and the organisation pays for oversight tools that do not change what is revealed.

### Five rules keep paid features from touching anonymity

**First, no paid feature may widen the circle of people who know an anonymous author.** Deputy controllers, an admin action that hands an existing group's review history to a new controller, and org-admin access to raw messages all let more people see who wrote what. That is the same risk family as NGL's hints, however it is packaged. **Second, no per-user perk may be visible on an anonymous message.** The backend already follows this rule for tone: anonymous messages always use the default professional tone, so a distinctive style cannot point to the writer ([API.md §44](../API.md); [migration 013](../backend/migrations/013_rewrite_tone.sql)). The same logic applies to a Plus badge, a longer character limit, larger attachments or priority processing. Any of these, attached to an anonymous message, narrows the possible authors to that group's paying members. In a ten-person group with one subscriber, it names the author. Individual perks therefore belong in the named feed and on the user's own screens. **Third, no free feature moves behind the paywall.** The go-to-market Team tier lists "custom tone", but per-user tone is already free. The startup plan's paid tier lists "summaries", but Catch up summaries are already free for every member ([API.md §63](../API.md); [GO_TO_MARKET.md](../docs/GO_TO_MARKET.md); [STARTUP_PLAN.md](../docs/STARTUP_PLAN.md)). Snap, after charging for storage that used to be free, publicly admitted how hard that switch is for users ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/29/snapchat-caps-free-memory-storage-launches-paid-storage-plans)). **Fourth, the safety pipeline is the same for everyone.** The AI rewrite is part of the safety promise, so "priority rewrites", which the go-to-market plan lists for Team, would make tone moderation slower for free groups and should be dropped. The same plan also suggests paid-only API fallbacks for AI. If raw (possibly anonymous) text is ever sent to a third-party model, that is a privacy-disclosure decision, not a perk. **Fifth, insights use aggregates only.** They are built from published text, shown only for groups of at least five people (the k-anonymity floor the go-to-market plan already proposes), never broken down by person, and advisory only.

These rules run into the product's own v1 scope, and that limits what a controller tier can sell. `AGENTS.md` excludes from v1 the features a reviewer tier would naturally charge for: auto-approve, deputies, timeouts, bulk approve, editing the rewrite, rejection reasons, and any "audit log or controller accountability UI" ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). The go-to-market Business tier still lists a "controller audit log" ([GO_TO_MARKET.md](../docs/GO_TO_MARKET.md)), which conflicts with that rule. Showing org admins how fast each controller reviews would also count as accountability UI. Insights v1 should therefore show review turnaround to the controller only. For v1, paid controller value has to come from insight, digest, templates and administration, not from new review powers.

### Organisations pay for capacity, insight and admin control

The evidence for what organisations pay for is consistent. Collaboration tools gate on capacity and history. Slack Free keeps 90 days of history and 10 apps ([Slack India pricing](https://slack.com/intl/en-in/pricing)). Polly's free tier keeps 45 days of results ([Polly pricing](https://www.polly.ai/pricing)). Slido Basic allows 100 participants ([Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/154051/Slido/pricing/)) and Mentimeter Free 50 a month ([Mentimeter plans](https://www.mentimeter.com/plans)). AI analysis of feedback is the core of engagement suites: 15Five Engage sells AI-synthesised open-text feedback ([15Five pricing](https://www.15five.com/pricing)), Lattice sells "AI Analysis & Trends" ([Lattice pricing](https://lattice.com/pricing)), and Workleap sells AI summaries ([Workleap pricing](https://workleap.com/pricing)). Slack raised Business+ from $12.50 to $15 while bundling AI recaps and search ([Slack blog](https://slack.com/blog/news/june-2025-pricing-and-packaging-announcement)). Admin controls sit in higher tiers: SAML single sign-on (SSO) and an API in Workleap Pro, branding in Mentimeter Pro, and an admin dashboard in Polly Enterprise (sources above). Workleap's price of $4,999 plus **$32 per manager per month** shows that some vendors charge per manager, because only the people running the system use the paid tools ([Workleap pricing](https://workleap.com/pricing)). History limits are the one lever to avoid. Talk It Out already keeps history free, and when Slack swapped its free plan's message cap for a 90-day window, a competitor, Zulip, reported teams leaving ([Zulip blog](https://blog.zulip.com/2022/08/26/why-slacks-free-plan-change-is-causing-an-exodus/)).

### An individual tier can only live in the named feed

Consumer apps sell four things: status and cosmetics (Telegram, Discord, Reddit, X), higher limits, ad removal, and increasingly AI ([Telegram FAQ](https://telegram.org/faq_premium); [discord.com/nitro](https://discord.com/nitro); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/x-premium-price-increase-grok-3)). Talk It Out has no ads to remove, and rule two keeps perks out of group chats. That leaves the named Q&A feed and the user's own screens. Three Plus features fit. In rank order: **AI writing help for named questions and answers** (LinkedIn's AI profile tools are the closest proof that people pay for AI writing help); **personal statistics on your own questions and answers** (reach, votes and best-answer counts over time, an insight category LinkedIn sells and Reddit gives publishers free ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/reddit-launches-tools-for-publisher-to-track-and-share-stories/))); and **feed-only profile flair**. Plus must never reveal information that free users cannot see about other people, such as who viewed or who voted. Paying to learn more about others belongs in the same risk family as NGL. Every item here has a weak revenue outlook in India, which is why Plus belongs in the last phase.

### Free limits and trials should push teams, not punish them

The free plan should keep the hypothesis caps of **3 groups per workspace and 10 members per group**, with unlimited history, the full safety stack and the whole public feed. Existing beta groups should keep their current size permanently. Most of the target startups (20–200 people) exceed ten members per group, so the cap is a natural upgrade trigger. The go-to-market plan offers its 14-day trial "when someone creates their second group", which contradicts a free plan that allows three ([GO_TO_MARKET.md](../docs/GO_TO_MARKET.md)). The trigger should be the cap itself: creating a fourth group or inviting an eleventh member.

On trial design, card-required SaaS trials convert far better: ChartMogul reports 25–35% against 4–6% ([Userpilot](https://userpilot.com/blog/how-to-increase-free-trial-conversion/)). But one self-reported dataset shows card-required sign-ups at only about 30% of the no-card volume ([Visionary Marketing](https://visionary-marketing.co.uk/blog/saas-free-trial-conversion-statistics-2026)). Requiring a UPI mandate at sign-up adds friction exactly when the product most needs pilots. Team trials should therefore need no card. They should last 14 days, with 30-day pilots offered in person by the founder, because group value only appears after a retro cycle. RevenueCat's consumer data also favours longer trials: 17–32-day trials convert 42.5%, against 25.5% for trials of four days or fewer ([RevenueCat SOSA 2026](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2026/)). When a trial ends, the workspace should become read-only above the free limits and nothing should be deleted. That avoids the history-loss backlash entirely.

### UPI's ₹15,000 ceiling decides how annual plans get paid

India's 2026 e-mandate framework requires a **pre-debit notice at least 24 hours** before each charge. It lets customers pause or revoke mandates, and it requires additional authentication on recurring debits above **₹15,000** ([RBI Master Direction](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasDirections.aspx?id=13374); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/rbi-caps-recurring-payments-at-rs15000-without-otp-under-new-e-mandate-framework-526759-2026-04-21)). UPI cannot debit more than ₹15,000 off-session at all ([Stripe: India recurring payments](https://docs.stripe.com/india-recurring-payments?integration=subscriptions)). B2B prices in India are normally quoted excluding GST (Teams Essentials says "GST extra"), so the charge is ₹175.82 per monthly seat and ₹1,401.84 per annual seat. Monthly auto-debit therefore works up to about **85 seats**. Annual auto-debit works only up to **10 seats**. Larger annual plans should be billed by invoice (bank transfer or a payment link), which buyers who want GST invoices for input-tax credit prefer anyway. The first five to ten customers should be invoiced by hand before any checkout is built, as the go-to-market plan already advises.

## Selling nothing inside the apps keeps both stores simple until Plus ships

### Apple's two exceptions and Google's consumption-only carve-out

Apple's guideline 3.1.1 requires in-app purchase (IAP) to unlock any digital feature or subscription inside an iOS app. It explicitly bans unlocking through the developer's own mechanisms such as license keys or QR codes ([Apple Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Two exceptions matter. Under **3.1.3(b), Multiplatform Services**, users may access in the app what they bought on the web, provided the same items are also sold as IAP. Under **3.1.3(c), Enterprise Services**, organisation-bought access may be honoured without IAP, but only for an app "only sold directly by you to organizations or groups for their employees or students"; consumer sales must use IAP (same source). Outside the US storefront, the app and its listing may not include buttons, links or calls to action that steer users to other payment methods. Email and the website may still tell existing users about them. Google requires Play Billing for in-app digital sales and names "business productivity software" among them. It also states that **any app may be consumption-only**, meaning a user signs in to content paid for elsewhere and nothing is sold in the app. Such apps may say "Go to our website to upgrade your subscription to Premium" as long as they include no link ([Google Play Payments policy FAQ](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/10281818)).

That sets the sequence. **In phases 0–2 the apps sell nothing.** Android is a clean consumption-only app. On iOS, every sale is a Team plan made to an organisation on the web, which fits 3.1.3(c) as closely as Talk It Out can. Members simply sign in to a paid workspace. The existing invite links grant membership, not a paid unlock, so they stay clear of the license-key ban. **Once Plus ships as IAP**, the app sells to consumers and is no longer "only sold to organisations". Apple publishes nothing on whether 3.1.3(c) still covers one B2B plan inside a mixed app, and Google's text does not address apps that sell IAP and also honour web-bought seats. So Phase 3 should add a **Team in-app bundle** (for example "Team, up to 10 members", priced to cover the store commission) at the same moment, which keeps the web plan within 3.1.3(b). Apple's text sets no equal-pricing requirement for 3.1.3(b). Building Plus has a hidden cost: it pulls Team into the stores. There is one more grey zone. A hobby community that is neither employer nor school may look like a consumer group to Apple, so web checkout should collect an organisation name and issue invoices.

### US link-outs are open but revocable, and India's cases are unresolved

Since May 2025, apps on the **US storefront only** may link to web checkout ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/02/apple-changes-us-app-store-rules-to-let-apps-redirect-users-to-their-own-websites-for-payments/)), and Apple reportedly charges no commission on those purchases while the case continues. In December 2025 the Ninth Circuit upheld the contempt finding against Apple but allowed a future cost-based fee, to be set on remand ([SCL LLP](https://scl-llp.com/ninth-circuit-upholds-apple-contempt-finding-but-narrows-scope-of-remedial-relief/)). The Supreme Court then granted review on 30 June 2026, reportedly on one question only (this rests on a single search snippet). Apple filed its merits brief on 14 September 2026, and no decision had been reported as of late November ([Apple merits brief](https://www.supremecourt.gov/DocketPDF/25/25-1311/424150/20260914154510009_2026-09-14%20No.%2025-1311%20Apple-Epic%20Merits%20Opening%20Brief.pdf)). The US is not the main market and the rule could reverse, so **Talk It Out should not build US link-outs yet.** In India, nothing has been settled. NCLAT cut the competition regulator's (CCI's) Play Billing penalty from ₹936.44 crore to about ₹216.69 crore ([CompetitionLawyer.in](https://www.competitionlawyer.in/google-gets-some-relief-in-google-play-billing-case-in-appeal-nclats-reduces-penalties-and-rejects-ex-ante-regulation-by-cci/)). The Supreme Court of India heard developers' appeals on 3 November 2026 without a final ruling ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/11/223-google-play-billing-case-supreme-court/)). The CCI's App Store case against Apple is pending ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/05/19/india-refuses-to-let-apple-pause-app-store-case/)). Any of these could loosen steering rules in India later. None does today.

Commissions favour the web, though by less than the headline rates suggest. Apple takes **15% under the Small Business Program**, which requires enrolment. Without it, Apple takes 30% in a subscriber's first year and 15% after ([Apple Small Business Program](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program/); [Apple subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). Google takes **15% on subscriptions in India** regardless of revenue. User Choice Billing in India cuts that by 4 points, to about 11%, but adds gateway fees and RBI compliance work ([Google Play service fees](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/112622)). That saving is too small to justify the work, so Play Billing alone is the right default for Plus. Razorpay costs about 2% plus GST on the fee ([Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)).

### Every paywall needs the same disclosure checklist

Both stores require the same disclosures. Apple wants the plan name and duration, what the user gets, and the full renewal price as the most prominent pricing element. It wants trial length and post-trial price, a restore or sign-in path, links to Terms and Privacy, and easy access to the system cancel screen ([Apple subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). Before showing an offer, the app must also check whether the user already subscribes (same source). That matters here because the same person could otherwise buy Plus twice, once on the web and once in the app, which guideline 3.1.2(b) forbids. Google adds a cancellation link to the Play subscription centre. It also bans SKUs named "Free Trial" that charge automatically, and annual plans led by a monthly figure ([Google Play Subscriptions policy](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9900533)). In-app account deletion, which both stores require ([Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13327111?hl=en)), already exists in the profile screen. Google also wants a web deletion page, so its URL must be listed in the Play Console. On Android, about **31–32% of cancellations are billing failures**, against 14–15% on iOS ([RevenueCat 2026 trends](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/subscription-app-trends-benchmarks-2026.md)). Turning on Play's grace period and account hold is worth more than fine-tuning prices.

## The backend must own every entitlement, and it has no workspaces yet

### Razorpay for web seats, RevenueCat for store purchases

Two facts decide the stack. **New Indian businesses cannot sign up for Stripe without an invitation** ([Stripe Support](https://support.stripe.com/questions/stripe-accounts-are-invite-only-in-india)). **RevenueCat's own web billing does not support India-based customers or B2B sales**, and its Stripe integration supports neither UPI nor PayPal ([RevenueCat Web overview](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/overview.md); [RevenueCat Stripe integration](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/web/integrations/stripe.md)). The web Team plan therefore runs on **Razorpay Subscriptions**. It supports UPI Autopay and cards, has no setup fee, and bills by quantity (₹100 × 5 users = ₹500 in its own example). Quantity can change on a live subscription, and its add-ons feature is deprecated ([Razorpay Subscriptions FAQ](https://razorpay.com/docs/payments/subscriptions/faqs.md); [Razorpay pricing](https://razorpay.com/pricing/)). Store purchases, when Plus arrives, should run on RevenueCat's **purchases_flutter 11.0.4**. It requires Dart 3.4 or later (the app declares ^3.13.3), Kotlin 2.1+, compileSdk 35 and Play Billing Library 9.1.0. Its prebuilt paywalls in purchases_ui_flutter need iOS 15+ and Android 24+ ([pub.dev changelog](https://pub.dev/packages/purchases_flutter/changelog); [pub.dev purchases_ui_flutter](https://pub.dev/packages/purchases_ui_flutter)). RevenueCat is free below $2,500 in monthly tracked revenue and charges 1% above it ([RevenueCat pricing](https://www.revenuecat.com/pricing/)). Adapty (free under $5,000) and Qonversion (free to $7,000, then 0.8%) are cheaper ([Adapty pricing](https://adapty.io/pricing/); [Qonversion pricing](https://qonversion.io/pricing)), but every option is small next to a 15% store fee. The alternative, the official in_app_purchase plugin, has no web support and leaves all verification to the backend. The backend would have to verify Apple's signed transactions, acknowledge each Google purchase within three days or have it automatically refunded, take in App Store Server Notifications and Google's real-time notifications, and handle refunds ([pub.dev in_app_purchase](https://pub.dev/packages/in_app_purchase); [Android: Integrate Play Billing](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/integrate)). Apple's Node library for that also needs Node 22, while the backend declares Node 20 or later ([apple/app-store-server-library-node](https://github.com/apple/app-store-server-library-node)).

### A small schema, idempotent webhooks and server-side gates

The current schema has no concept of an organisation. Migrations 006–025 cover reactions, tokens, discovery, invites and the rest, but contain no workspace, seat or billing table ([backend/migrations](../backend/migrations)). Phase 1 therefore starts with a backend design. A `workspaces` table holds name, owner and optional GSTIN. `workspace_members` holds roles (owner, admin, member). Each group gains a `workspace_id`, and existing groups get an implicit free workspace owned by their controller. A `subscriptions` table records provider (`manual`, `razorpay`, `revenuecat`), owner type (user or workspace), status, period end, grace end and quantity. The research notes suggest storing status as text with an application-level enum, which matches the repo's existing rule of tolerating unknown values. A `billing_events` table keyed by provider and event ID makes webhook processing idempotent. A `user_entitlements` view combines a user's own Plus with the plan of every workspace they belong to. The `manual` provider covers the Phase 0 invoices, where the founder sets a plan by hand. Webhooks should return 200 at once and process the event afterwards, then re-fetch the provider's current state rather than trust the order events arrive in. RevenueCat delivers at least once, disconnects after 60 seconds, retries five times over about 80 minutes, and recommends calling `GET /subscribers` after each event ([RevenueCat webhooks](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/integrations/webhooks.md)). Razorpay checkout passes `org_id` and `user_id` in its `notes` field, so a webhook can find the workspace it belongs to. Limits must be enforced on the server at group creation, member add and invite acceptance, returning a `plan_limit` error code. A hidden button is not a limit. Billing data must also respect the existing privacy rules. Webhook payloads and billing logs never contain message text. Entitlements are never attached to message payloads. Seat usage is shown to admins as a count, never as a per-member activity list, because a list next to an anonymous message is a clue. `AGENTS.md` assigns `backend/` to another team, so this design needs that team's sign-off ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)).

### Flutter changes fit the existing repository pattern

On the client, billing becomes one more feature behind a repository interface: `BillingRepository` with `MockBillingRepository` and `ApiBillingRepository`, switched by `USE_MOCK` like every other data source. An `entitlementsProvider` (a Riverpod `AsyncNotifier`) reads `GET /me/entitlements`. Endpoint paths go in `endpoints.dart`, and all copy, including the store-mandated disclosures, goes in `app_strings.dart` ([AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md)). One widget should decide what an upgrade prompt shows on each platform. On web, it opens the Razorpay checkout. On Android in phases 0–2, it shows plain text pointing to the website, with no link. On iOS outside the US, it says only that billing is managed by the workspace admin. From Phase 3, the native paywall appears only for Plus and the Team bundle, and only after the entitlement check shows the user is not already subscribed. Because Apple requires a subscription to work on all of a user's devices ([Apple Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)), the app calls `Purchases.logIn(<backend user id>)` after sign-in. Even then, the backend's answer always wins over RevenueCat's on-device data, since only the backend knows about Razorpay seats. Each new dependency must be recorded in `LOGS.md`.

### Testing and store setup carry hard deadlines

The purchases_flutter changelog warns that **CocoaPods trunk becomes permanently read-only on 2 December 2026** and recommends Swift Package Manager ([pub.dev changelog](https://pub.dev/packages/purchases_flutter/changelog)). That date is six days away, and it affects the iOS build whether or not IAP ships. RevenueCat provides a Test Store that needs no App Store Connect or Play Console access, which suits early work ([RevenueCat sandbox testing](https://www.revenuecat.com/docs/test-and-launch/sandbox.md)). Real store testing still needs hardware this Mac lacks: a physical Android device for Play license testers and a working Xcode for TestFlight. On Play, testers who are not license testers are charged real money. TestFlight subscriptions renew daily, up to six times a week. Apple's sandbox sends each server notification only once ([Android: Test Play Billing](https://developer.android.com/google/play/billing/test); [Apple TestFlight](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/test-a-beta-version/testing-subscriptions-and-in-app-purchases-in-testflight); [Apple notifications](https://developer.apple.com/tutorials/data/documentation/appstoreservernotifications/responding-to-app-store-server-notifications.json)). Selling on iOS requires signing the Paid Apps Agreement, filing a US tax form, adding banking details and then enrolling in the Small Business Program ([App Store Connect Help](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/provide-tax-information/tax-forms-overview)). On Google Play, Indian developers determine their own GST position and provide a GSTIN if they are registered ([Google Payments Center help](https://support.google.com/paymentscenter/answer/7421525)).

## Conclusion

The individual tier is weaker than it first looks, for three separate reasons. It earns little in India. Every per-user perk risks narrowing down who wrote an anonymous message. And the first consumer purchase pulls the Team plan into store billing too. The cheapest route to revenue is also the safest: organisations pay, on the web, for tools that read only published text, only in aggregate.

The real limit on what Talk It Out can charge controllers for is not the market but the product's own safety scope. The features controllers will ask for first are deputies, bulk approval, an audit trail and controller reassignment. Each one changes who sees what, and each needs a safety decision before it can be a product at all, let alone a paid one. Price is the least certain part of this plan. No source measured Indian conversion at different price points, so ₹149/₹99 should be tested on the first ten teams with hand-made invoices before any checkout code is written.
